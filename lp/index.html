Push notifications that never leave your infrastructure.

push-hub is a self-hosted push notification delivery engine for enterprises. Deploy it inside your own VPC or on-premises, and send APNs / FCM notifications without handing your data to a SaaS provider.

The problem

Push SaaS is easy to start with, and hard to justify as you grow.

01

Data sovereignty and compliance

Customer behavior logs, notification bodies, and device tokens are stored on an external provider's servers, often in another country.

02

The "SaaS Tax"

Pay-per-use pricing grows with delivery volume, and becomes a major cost at millions to hundreds of millions of notifications per month.

03

Vendor lock-in

Your delivery pipeline, device data, and analytics depend on a single provider.

Who it's for

Built for teams that can't, or won't, hand push data to a third party.

B2B enterprises and security-focused startups

Healthcare, finance, SaaS, government, and similar sectors that cannot send customer data or push tokens to external SaaS providers.

Development and DevOps teams at scale

Teams delivering millions to hundreds of millions of push notifications per month who want predictable costs.

Data governance by design

push-hub runs entirely inside your infrastructure.

  • Your data stays in your database.Device tokens, recipient attributes, notification content, delivery logs, and analytics live in your own PostgreSQL.
  • The only outbound traffic goes to APNs and FCM.No telemetry, usage statistics, or crash reports are sent anywhere.
  • No license server.push-hub never phones home to check a license, so it also works in air-gapped environments.
  • Runs on any cloud or on-premises.The only required dependency is PostgreSQL. No cloud-specific services are used.
  • Standard observability.Metrics, logs, and health checks plug into Prometheus, Grafana, Datadog, CloudWatch, or ELK.

What push-hub cannot change: to deliver a notification, its content and the device token must be sent to Apple (APNs) or Google (FCM). This is true for every push provider, including SaaS. push-hub removes the additional third party in between, but it does not remove Apple or Google from the path.

Architecture

A single binary and PostgreSQL. That's it.

Your application server calls the REST API, api enqueues a job in PostgreSQL, and worker sends it to APNs / FCM. scheduler handles scheduled deliveries.

Quick start · preview

One docker compose up.

No image has been published yet. This shows the planned startup experience and does not work today. For production, a Helm chart with a managed PostgreSQL is planned.

services:
  postgres:
    image: postgres:17
  push-hub:
    image: ghcr.io/aianchor-lab/push-hub:latest
    command: ["all"] # api + worker + scheduler
    ports: ["8080:8080"]
    environment:
      PUSHHUB_DATABASE_URL: postgres://pushhub:pushhub@postgres:5432/pushhub
      PUSHHUB_APNS_KEY_FILE: /run/secrets/apns.p8
      PUSHHUB_FCM_CREDENTIALS_FILE: /run/secrets/fcm.json
$ docker compose up -d
$ curl http://localhost:8080/healthz
{"status":"ok"}
